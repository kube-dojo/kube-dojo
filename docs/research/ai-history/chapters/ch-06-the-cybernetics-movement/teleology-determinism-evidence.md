## Inspected source

Arturo Rosenblueth, Norbert Wiener and Julian Bigelow, “Behavior, Purpose and Teleology,” *Philosophy of Science* 10(1), January1943, pp.18–24. University-hosted scan: https://home.csulb.edu/~cwallis/382/readings/482/wiener.behavior.purpose.teleology.1943.pdf. Retrieved664132bytes; SHA256 `4369ef758fe4d8055a2a39db8d3595d92fab73118de91e6b1b040c467f73cca9`; eight PDF pages including a JSTOR cover. Lead visually inspected PDF7–8, printed23–24. The web text search missed the determinism passage; the rendered original pages establish it.

Printed23, paragraph beginning “In classifying behavior,” explains the authors' restricted use of teleology in terms of purpose controlled by feedback. The following paragraph, beginning “We have restricted the connotation,” continues on printed24, where they identify error-controlled purposeful reactions with negative feedback. Printed24's next paragraph explicitly distinguishes teleology from non-teleology, rather than opposing it to determinism. It conditionally allows both classes to be deterministic where determinism applies. This is a classification argument; it neither proves that all systems are deterministic nor supplies an experimental result about modern machines.
