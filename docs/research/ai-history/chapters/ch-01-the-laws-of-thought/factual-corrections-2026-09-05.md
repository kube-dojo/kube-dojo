- The [Stanford Encyclopedia entry](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/boole/), `#LifeWork` (secondary scholarly reference, recorded 2026-09-05), uses “Gold Medal.” That is a terminology conflict with the direct Royal Society catalogue record, not a reason to reopen whether an 1844 award is documented.

Access failure alone neither disproves the award nor satisfies independent review; use the neutral wording in prose until that review is resolved.

### Jevons, Peirce, and Schröder — `src/content/docs/ai-history/ch-01-the-laws-of-thought.md:123,125` (`Conflict` / partial support)

- [Burris, “George Boole,” SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/boole/#ObjeBoolAlgeLogi), §5.1 (secondary scholarly source, recorded 2026-09-05), dates the Boole–Jevons exchange to **1863**, not 1864. Jevons’s [*Pure Logic* record](https://archive.org/details/purelogicorlogi00jevogoog), title page (1864) and preface (November 1863), is a primary book scan/catalogue (recorded 2026-09-05). The [Jevons letters volume](https://oll-resources.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/oll3/store/titles/2079/Jevons_1357_EBk_v6.0.pdf), PDF p. 128 (edited correspondence and editorial narrative reproduced in the PLL e-book), records August 1863 correspondence and editorial publication in early 1864. Appendix B's list of writings, PDF p. 323, instead lists *Pure Logic* under 18 December 1863; this is a bibliographic entry, not a letter. The latter anchor was rechecked directly on 2026-09-05. Keep correspondence, title-page year, and the conflicting publication dates distinct; PDF locators here refer to the PLL edition, not original print pagination.
- The same SEP entry, `#ModeRecoBoolSyst` and `#BoolGeneMethForSecoProp` (secondary scholarly source), supports Benjamin Peirce’s 1870 naming of the idempotent law and later C. S. Peirce terminology. Its bibliography explicitly lists Schröder’s *Algebra der Logik*, Volumes I–III, as **1890–1910**, Leipzig: B. G. Teubner (HTML bibliography rechecked 2026-09-05). This is partial bibliographic attestation, not verification of each original volume’s publication date. It does not establish the chapter’s exact “late 1860s” relation/predicate chronology or “1890–95 three volumes.”
- **Disposition:** change the event date to “In 1863 Jevons proposed…” and separately identify the 1864 *Pure Logic* title page (preface November 1863). Preserve the publication-stage conflict and avoid an unqualified “within months” claim. Keep the exact Peirce chronology and individual Schröder volume dates `Unknown`; retain SEP’s aggregate bibliography as attributed partial support.
- **Mechanical bridge, source-file line 135:** the same letters volume, PDF pp. 164–165, contains Jevons’s **23 June 1868** letter to his brother Herbert describing a logical machine with 21 keys. The 18 April letter ends on PDF p. 164 before the June letter begins; the machine passage on p. 165 continues the June letter. This is Jevons’s self-report, not an independent performance test. **Disposition:** qualify the categorical “logical, not engineering” contrast: distinguish mechanical logic devices from the later electrical switching work, without claiming that this letter establishes either priority or reliable operation. The earlier #2302 comment assigning the machine passage to 18 April was incorrect.

- [MIT OCW combinational logic](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-004-computation-structures-spring-2017/pages/c4/c4s1/) (institutional teaching source, recorded 2026-09-05) supports Boolean representation of combinational truth-table functions with AND/OR/NOT (and functional equivalents). - **Disposition:** narrow the note to combinational Boolean functions and/or Shannon’s relay-switch result; change “all execute chains” to “often use Boolean expressions”; leave “another half-century” unquantified until sourced. The universal physical claim is not established.
