## Scene 1: The Vagabond Genius — Framed, Not Romanticised

- **Setting:** Detroit (1935 library scene, Lettvin oral history) and the University of Chicago campus (early 1940s).
- **Beat:** Walter Pitts's path from Detroit boiler-maker family into McCulloch's Hinsdale household runs through Bertrand Russell on two separate occasions: the 1935 *Principia* episode (reported only via Lettvin oral history) and the 1938 Chicago run-away to attend Russell's lectures (same chain). Lettvin introduces Pitts to McCulloch in the early 1940s. The chapter must hold its dramatic restraint here: the beats are too historically load-bearing to skip but too dependent on Lettvin's later retelling to assert as documentary.
- **Pedagogical move:** Frame the scenes explicitly as "Lettvin remembered…" or "in the version Lettvin preserved through oral history…" rather than third-person omniscient narration. Smalheiser 2000 — paywalled — would be the more measured biographical source; until physical-journal access lands, the chapter reads Gefter through Smalheiser-shaped hedges.
- **Narrative use:** Establishes Pitts as the technical capability that made the 1943 paper possible without committing the chapter to romance-isms about prodigy. The arc is "outsider with extraordinary symbolic-logic facility finds an institutional perch" — not "boy genius corrects Russell."
- **Evidence anchors:** Gefter 2015 *Nautilus* paragraph 2 (1935 library and Detroit family); paragraph 3 (3-day *Principia* read, the Russell letter, 1938 Chicago run-away); paragraph 7 (Lettvin's introduction of Pitts to McCulloch); Hinsdale paragraphs (Pitts moves into McCulloch's household). Smalheiser 2000 bibliographic anchor (paywalled, must remain Yellow). Conway-Siegelman 2005 bibliographic anchor (no page-level access yet).

## Scene 2: The Chicago Mathematical Biophysics Setting

- **Setting:** University of Chicago, early 1940s — but the scene is intellectual, not domestic. The chapter does not need a room; it needs a journal masthead, a reading list, and a community.
- **Beat:** McCulloch had been chasing a logical theory of nervous activity since his Yale and Columbia years, framed in his head as a Leibnizian "alphabet of thought" project (Gefter 2015 paragraph 7). Chicago had Nicolas Rashevsky's mathematical-biophysics group, publishing the *Bulletin of Mathematical Biophysics* — the journal that would eventually publish the 1943 paper. The biophysical assumptions about neurons (all-or-none firing, thresholds, synaptic delay) were already in the air there. What McCulloch lacked was the symbolic apparatus. Carnap's 1938 *Logical Syntax of Language* and the Russell-Whitehead *Principia* gave him a notation; Pitts gave him the technical capability to use it.
- **Pedagogical move:** This scene is the chapter's correction of the popular "first mathematical model of neurons" framing. Use Piccinini 2004's abstract claim ("in 1943 there already existed a lively community of biophysicists doing mathematical work on neural networks") and Hebb 1949's qualified community discussion (printed xi–xii, PDF pp.7–8: preliminary studies, simplification, and potential value awaiting further results) to ground the correction. The named list does not establish a head-of-community role or direct lineage. The novelty being introduced in the next scene is *symbolic logic on top of biophysics*, not *mathematics applied to neurons for the first time*.
- **Narrative use:** Sets up the chapter's central distinction (continuous biophysics vs. symbolic logic) so that Scene 3's Carnap-syntax walk-through has historical weight rather than reading as pure math instruction.
- **Evidence anchors:** Piccinini 2004 abstract (Springer-anchored); Hebb 1949 printed xi–xii (PDF pp.7–8; qualified community discussion and named list); M-P 1943 p.118 (in-text adoption of Carnap's Language II); M-P 1943 p.131 (literature list confirms Carnap 1938, Russell-Whitehead 1925, Hilbert-Ackermann 1927); Gefter 2015 paragraph 7 (McCulloch's Leibnizian project).

## Scene 3: The 1943 Paper, Read Slowly

- **Setting:** Inside the paper itself.
- **Beat:** A pedagogical walk-through of "A Logical Calculus of the Ideas Immanent in Nervous Activity" — the all-or-none assumption, the five physical assumptions of Section 2, the Carnap-syntax notation $N_i(t)$ for "neuron $i$ fires at time $t$" and the temporal-shift functor $S$, the threshold-logic gate constructions (AND at $\theta=2$; OR at $\theta=1$; conjoined negation: $N_3(t) \equiv N_1(t-1) \land \neg N_2(t-1)$, retaining both the excitatory condition and inactive inhibitory input at one synaptic delay), and the realizability results under their stated TPE/truth-table and extended-sense conditions, without implying a unique net.
- **Pedagogical move:** This is the math-teaching core. Reproduce one or two threshold-logic gate constructions from the paper itself; do not invent a generic toy network. The reader should leave with the ability to read the abstract claim "neural events and the relations among them can be treated by means of propositional logic" (M-P 1943 p.115) as an actual mathematical statement, not a slogan.
- **Narrative use:** The chapter earns its length here. If the reader cannot follow Scene 3 in plain prose, the chapter has failed regardless of word count.
- **Evidence anchors:** M-P 1943 p.115 (abstract); p.117 ("Many years ago one of us…" passage, mapping each neural firing to a proposition); p.118 (five physical assumptions; Language II of Carnap citation; $N_i(t)$ and the functor $S$); pp.118-124 (theorems for nets without circles; conjunction/disjunction/conjoined-negation gate constructions). All verified by `pdftotext` 2026-04-28.

## Scene 4: What the Paper Actually Proved, and Said About Learning

- **Setting:** Continuing inside the paper, into Section 3 ("Nets with Circles") and Section 4 ("Consequences").
- **Beat:** The visible reprint p.108 places Theorem 7 ("Alterable synapses can be replaced by circles") before the Section 3 heading. The paragraph immediately before it assumes an activity-dependent alteration in which initially ineffective axonal terminations become ordinary excitatory synapses when their excitation coincides with firing of the succeeding neuron. Visible p.101 distinguishes the formal fixed-net equivalence from a factual explanation of physiological change.
- **Pedagogical move:** This scene is the chapter's defence against the flat "model has no learning mechanism" framing. The bounded version is sharper: the paper states an alteration rule and gives a formal fixed-net representation, but that representation does not establish successful training for a chosen task. Do not generalise it to all biological learning or all changing neural networks.
- **Narrative use:** Forces the reader to read the 1943 paper as more sophisticated than the textbook compression while keeping formal representation separate from successful task training and biological explanation. It sets up later learning research without making a first-algorithm priority claim.
- **Evidence anchors:** M-P 1943 visible reprint p.101 (learning discussion and formal-equivalence distinction); visible reprint p.108 (explicit alteration rule and Theorem 7 before the Section 3 heading); later Section 3 and Section 4 material remains locator-sensitive. Do not substitute inferred original-page numbers for these visible locators.

## Scene 5: The Hebbian Bridge — Without Calling It an Algorithm

- **Setting:** Hebb's *Organization of Behavior*, published six years later (Wiley, 1949).
- **Beat:** Hebb's Introduction (printed xi–xii, PDF pp.7–8) gives a qualified discussion of preliminary mathematical work: the studies simplified the psychological problem, and their potential value awaited further results. The named list does not establish a head-of-community role or direct lineage. Hebb's contribution is biological: Chapter 4 introduces the assembly proposal at printed p.60 and states the "neurophysiological postulate" at p.62 — "When an axon of cell A is near enough to excite a cell B and repeatedly or persistently takes part in firing it, some growth process or metabolic change takes place in one or both cells such that A's efficiency, as one of the cells firing B, is increased." At p.63 he says the particular synaptic-knob mechanism under discussion has no direct evidence. The chapter must read this as a *biological hypothesis* about where plasticity might live in the nervous system, not as a weight-update rule on a McCulloch-Pitts net. Later naming and mathematical development of rules called Hebbian require separate historical evidence. These inspected pages do not support a whole-book absence claim about equations, time scales, or quantitative rules.
- **Pedagogical move:** Be precise about what Hebb did and did not give us. The postulate is not a training procedure for a chosen task, and this chapter does not decide which later researcher first supplied an algorithm. Ch14 can discuss Rosenblatt separately.
- **Narrative use:** Closes the chapter's main arc by naming the gap that the next two decades would try to close. Sets up Ch14 (perceptron) and the Ch24 backpropagation arc without anticipating their arguments.
- **Honest close (within Scene 5, ~300-500 words):** Piccinini 2004's abstract attributes four contributions to 1943; Kleene's 1956 treatment places McCulloch-Pitts nerve nets inside a finite-state automaton framework under stated finite-state and initial-state conditions, without establishing direct causal lead or priority. The von Neumann 1945 *First Draft of a Report on the EDVAC* supplies narrower direct evidence: §4.2 cites M-P 1943 across printed pp.12–13 (PDF pp.35,37), and the next paragraph says the simplified neuron functions can be imitated with telegraph relays or vacuum tubes before §4.3. Gefter 2015's "cites only" wording remains an attributed secondary account, not a verified citation census. Do not treat this evidence as proving sole architectural authorship or priority, broad influence or mediation, or a fixed two-and-a-half-year interval. The 1959 frog-eye paper is an out-of-scope forward-pointer: its inspected summary reports local optic-nerve responses, and its General Discussion says information reaching the brain has already undergone organization in the eye. Do not infer Pitts's private reaction, dissertation destruction, or a refutation of the 1943 calculus; Gefter's reaction narrative remains a separate attribution. The chapter ends with the stance that 1943 named a useful level of abstraction without establishing a procedure for training a chosen task.
- **Evidence anchors:** Hebb 1949 printed xi–xii (PDF pp.7–8; qualified community discussion); printed pp.60 and 62 (Chapter 4 framing; postulate); p.63 (no direct evidence for the particular anatomical mechanism); Piccinini 2004 abstract (Springer-anchored, attributed four-contribution record); Kleene 1956 reproduction pp.31,37,40 (finite-state and initial-state conditions); von Neumann 1945 *First Draft* printed pp.12–13, PDF pp.35,37, §4.2 and following paragraph (**Green for direct citation only** via page-image review 2026-09-05); Lettvin et al. 1959 frog-eye scan PDF pp.1,11 (printed pp.1940,1950); Gefter 2015 remains a secondary attribution for reaction claims; M-P 1943 pp.130-131 (Section 4 "Consequences" close).
