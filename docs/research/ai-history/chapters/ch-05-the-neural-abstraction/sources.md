## Conflict Notes

- **"First mathematical model of neurons" framing.** Several popular accounts and the legacy Gemini-drafted Ch5 prose imply M-P 1943 was the first mathematical model of the neuron. Piccinini 2004 abstract corrects this; Hebb 1949 printed xi–xii (PDF pp.7–8) gives a qualified list of a community whose preliminary studies simplified the psychological problem and whose potential value awaited further results. The list does not establish that Rashevsky headed the community or a direct lineage. The chapter must use "first symbolic-logic / first computational theory of the neuron" framing.
- **"No learning mechanism" framing.** The legacy prose says the 1943 model "had no mechanism for learning." Visible reprint p.101 discusses learning and formal equivalence; p.108 supplies the explicit activity-dependent alteration rule and places Theorem 7 before Section 3. The chapter must distinguish that specified alteration rule and formal representation from successful training for a chosen task, without generalising to all biological learning or all changing neural networks.
- **"Hebb's rule was the missing learning algorithm" framing.** Common textbook compression. Hebb 1949 printed p.62 states a *biological* postulate, following the assembly proposal at p.60, not a training procedure; p.63 says the particular anatomical mechanism under discussion has no direct evidence. Claims about equations, time scales, or quantitative rules must remain bounded to the inspected passage rather than generalized to the whole book. First-learning-algorithm priority remains open, and Ch14 may discuss Rosenblatt separately.
- **The Pitts-as-twelve-year-old anecdote.** Sourced through Lettvin oral history (*Talking Nets* 2000) and rendered in vivid prose by Gefter 2015. Smalheiser 2000 — the most careful biographical source — is paywalled. The chapter must mark these scenes as Lettvin-oral-history reconstructions and resist asserting precise dates and page counts as documentary.
- **In-text vs literature-list bibliographic dates for Russell-Whitehead.** M-P 1943 p.118 cites "Russell and Whitehead (1927)"; the literature list at p.131 cites "Russell, B. and A. N. Whitehead. 1925. Principa Mathematics." Both refer to the *Principia* second edition (whose three volumes appeared 1925-1927); the first edition is 1910-1913. Use 1925 for the cited second edition.
