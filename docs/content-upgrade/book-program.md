## Claim-level evidence and fact-check gate

Every load-bearing claim gets one row in the chapter's evidence matrix, sourced from the chapter contract and the shared [source catalog](../research/ai-history/source-catalog.md):

`claim_id | chapter/section | exact claim | source tier (primary/secondary/tertiary) | citation/stable ID | source locator (page, section, figure, table, theorem, paragraph, or archival identifier) | checked_on (YYYY-MM-DD) | supported scope (who/what/where/when) | dates, ranges, units, denominator, and precision | conflicts/uncertainty | Green/Yellow/Red | reviewer`

One row must not hide multiple assertions. Preserve source wording where dates or quantities disagree; record the conflict in `brief.md` rather than averaging it. Distinguish announcement from operation, planned capacity from delivered capacity, and estimate from measurement. Modern claims, legal matters, prices, model releases, and infrastructure counts require a fresh checked date and a bounded time scope. Green means the exact assertion is anchored; Yellow means partial, disputed, or secondary support and must be hedged; Red means unsupported or out of scope and must be removed or left as an open question.

Fact-check readiness is separate from editorial readiness. The fact-check gate is: complete contract, concrete gap analysis, extracted primary anchors, independent corroboration required by the chapter's sourcing contract, conflict notes, and the cross-family readiness verdicts required by TEAM_WORKFLOW.md. The editorial gate is: an evidence-bounded capacity plan, coherent structure, natural word range, source-fidelity review, prose-quality review, and accountable-lead integration. Use `researching` through `prose_ready` statuses honestly; `accepted` requires the recorded review evidence. No source list alone proves claim verification.

## Pacing, aids, and optional teaching

For editorial revisions, use the evidence-supported shape: documented problem, people or institutions involved, constraints, key idea, accessible explanation, and what changed or remained unresolved. Vary the form by chapter. Tighten repeated theses and add headings where narrative beats warrant them; never add a scene, motive, quotation, dialogue, or chapter merely to hit a word target. The capacity plan records a core range and a stretch range; if evidence runs out, cap the chapter. Do not turn reading into a mystery or discovery gimmick.

Reader aids follow a separate additive workflow with existing prose bit-identical. Opening/closing aids surround the prose; approved Tier 3 asides or pull-quotes may be inserted at their specified paragraph anchors without rewriting the paragraph. Review applicability to Ch73 while reconciling READER_AIDS.md. Its existing Tier 1 limits are: an ≤80-word TL;DR, a six-row-or-fewer cast from `people.md`, an in-scope timeline from `timeline.md`, a 5–7-term plain-words glossary, and a ≤120-word closing note. Tier 2 remains selective: math aids for Ch01, Ch04, Ch15, Ch24, Ch25, Ch27, Ch29, Ch44, Ch50, Ch55, Ch58; architecture sketches for Ch41, Ch42, Ch49, Ch50, Ch52, Ch58. Tier 3 requires a proposal and adversarial cross-family approve/reject/revise record; tooltips remain skipped until a non-destructive component exists. Every aid claim traces to the contract, with only the high-level closing note allowed a carefully bounded forward bridge.

Optional teaching examples are simulations, never historical events: move points to expose the perceptron's linear boundary (Ch14/17); choose a gradient step and predict the next point (Ch15/24); compare a tiny attention table (Ch50); visualize vector arithmetic (Ch44); denoise a toy signal (Ch58); or change a benchmark distribution and inspect the conclusion (Ch66). Label each as a teaching example, state objective and limitation, provide a text/table fallback and keyboard access, and do not imply that a historical actor used it. Validate aids with `git diff main -- src/content/docs/ai-history/ch-XX-*.md | grep '^-[^-]'` so no prose line changes under the additive workflow.
